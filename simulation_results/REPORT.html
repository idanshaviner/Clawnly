How a day works: code builds a pool of eligible people per activity and time. Opus plans the groups and messages each person's agent one to one. Findings, most serious first

What the audit found

Each card opens to show what happened, why it matters, the fix, and the logged events behind it. Event numbers like T1 #560 point into that run's full_transcript.txt.

Agents talked themselves past their person's limits

HighFixed, verified Two agents accepted a 9:30pm hockey game for people who avoid late nights. One made up a reason.
What
Code told both agents the event runs past 9pm and their person avoids late nights. Both said yes and were locked in. Ava's agent called 9:30pm "not a late night". Nikhil's agent claimed he had "no early morning after", which his calendar doesn't show, and two days earlier the same agent had asked for a 9pm event to end at 8pm.
Why
The agent is the person's only voice. It has no memory between days, so nothing stops it contradicting itself.
Fix
One line in the agent's instructions: what the person avoids is a limit they set, not a preference to trade off.
Proof
The exact two decisions replayed 10 times with each version. Old instructions: yes 10 of 10. New: no 10 of 10.
Events
T1 #560 #562 #573 #578 #274 #477

The planner couldn't see what people avoid

MediumFixed Late-night avoiders were invited to late games because the planner only saw people's likes.
What
Grace, who avoids late nights, was invited to the 7–9:30pm game and declined. Ava and Nikhil (above) were invited the same way.
Why
Wasted invitations, smaller groups, and the setup for the problem above.
Fix
The planner now sees what each person avoids, and is told to check it against fixed event times.
Result
After the fix, nobody was sent a plan that clashed with what they avoid (it happened 4 times before).

Groups described by interests their members don't have

MediumPartly fixed "Five trivia-keen neighbors" when one of the five liked trivia. Seen in 6 groups.
What
People join an activity's pool through any interest it touches, such as the beer at a trivia night. The planner saw only a keenness score and read it as fandom. In the town run, real fans were left as backups while side-interest members filled the group (trivia, birding, a Seahawks party, cribbage).
Fix
The planner now sees which of the activity's interests each person has, and is told to describe only real ones.
Result
Descriptions are now accurate: "one genuine trivia buff to carry the team and three craft-beer fans along for the pints". Who gets chosen didn't measurably change: about a quarter of placements are still for a side interest, the same as the code-only test.
Open
Team question Q1: should liking the main activity be required?

Confirmation messages invented shared interests

MediumMostly fixed "Quinn shares your love of live music." He doesn't. This is the one message people actually read.
What
Messages claimed shared interests that weren't there: Quinn and live music, Wei and history, Ron and crafts.
Fix
Code now works out what each group really shares, and the message writer may only claim shared interests from that list.
Result
No made-up shared interests after the fix. What remains is milder exaggeration, like "loves" for someone who is only "up for" it.
Events
P1 #326 #336 #482

The same people get picked day after day

MediumOpen: team decision Over three days only 82 of 200 people were asked. Parents were passed over most. Details below.
What
104 people who fitted at least one activity were never asked. 4 people were placed all three days and 32 twice. Whole popular activities got no group.
Why
Each day is planned on its own with no memory of who was placed yesterday, the planner sees the same keenest people at the top of each pool every day, and there are 10 groups a day.
Next
Team question Q6: should the planner favor people placed least? The smallest change would show the planner "placed N times this week". Not changed here because it decides who gets matched.
Events
T1 #533 #755 and the fairness table in T1's audit_report.md

Round two used for pointless "confirmed!" messages

MediumFixed 20 extra agent calls in one day, and once telling a group who had declined.
What
The planner's only second-round action is "ask", so it re-asked everyone who had already said yes just to confirm. Once it told two people "Grace can't make it", which shares her private answer.
Fix
Instructions say to ask only for answers still needed and never name decliners; code also skips re-asking a settled yes.
Result
Agent calls in the pilot dropped from 69 to 43 and cost from $0.51 to $0.43.

Smaller fixes and loose ends

LowMixed A data bug, two rule gaps now enforced in code, and claims about distance with no data behind them.
Fixed
4 of the 200 emulated parents liked the same thing twice, which confused their agents and doubled their keenness score. Merged without changing anyone else in the town.
Guarded
Two rules the planner was only told to follow are now enforced by code: a group's time must stay inside its part of the day, and anyone who said yes to an old time is asked again after a move. Neither ever went wrong live (4 time moves, all handled well).
Open
The planner says venues are "close by" or "no long drive" with no distance data. Either add distances or stop the claim.

What still needs attention

Next steps

  1. Decide Q1 and Q6 as a team. Q1: should the main interest be required? Q6: should the planner favor people placed least this week? These drive the two open findings.
  2. Run one more paid stage with busier people. After the fixes every agent said yes at once, so declines, negotiation and the new code checks haven't been exercised live. About $1 to $2.
  3. Bring the browser copy of the matchmaker in line. The standalone published page uses its own copy, which doesn't have these fixes yet. Then republish it.
  4. Give the planner travel distances, or stop it claiming places are "close by".
  5. Push the branch when the team has reviewed it.

The audit cost more than the simulations: Fable 5.1 is about 2.5 times Opus per token. The matchmaker itself costs about $0.35 to $0.40 a day for 200 people at 10 groups. Every paid step had a hard ceiling, and none could start with more than what was left of the $10.

How far to trust this

Limits

  • A 95–100% yes rate isn't proof of quality: the pools already exclude hard conflicts and the planner picks the keenest people.
  • Before-and-after samples are small (12 groups before, 8 after) and the models vary run to run, so we compare rates, not totals.
  • The auditor makes mistakes too. Once it called a decline unforeseeable that its own group review showed was foreseeable. Every finding links to evidence, but none is ground truth.
  • The town is synthetic: calendars repeat by weekday and there are no travel distances.
  • Only 2 of the town's 30 groups got a full auditor review (the ceiling stopped the rest); all 3 town days were reviewed as a whole.
